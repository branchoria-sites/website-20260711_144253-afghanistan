## Introduction

The thylacine, often called the Tasmanian tiger, occupies a unique place in [Australia]({{ 'what-lurks-in-australias-strange-animal/' | relative_url }})’s mystery-animal tradition because it was not a legend at all. It was a real predator, photographed, filmed and studied before disappearing from the wild. The last known captive animal died in Hobart in 1936, yet reports of surviving thylacines have continued ever since. That unusual history makes modern sightings far more compelling to many people than ordinary monster stories: witnesses are not claiming to see an unknown beast, but a known animal that may have escaped extinction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/extinction-of-thylacine" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nma.gov.au">[National Museum of Australia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nma.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">extinction of thylacine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Museum of AustraliaExtinction of thylacine9 Sept 2025 — On 7 September 1936 the last known thylacine died at Beaumaris Zoo in Ho...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1936</span></span></span>

## Could the Tasmanian Tiger Still Be Out There?

Unlike many cryptids, the thylacine’s mystery begins after its accepted extinction. Officially, the species is regarded as extinct, but reports never truly stopped. Government wildlife agencies in Tasmania have continued to record sightings, and many witnesses insist they observed an animal with the distinctive striped hindquarters, stiff tail and unusual gait associated with the thylacine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2019-10-16/thylacine-sightings-in-tasmania-revealed-in-rti/11602970" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">thylacine sightings in tasmania revealed in rti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsTasmanian tiger sightings detailed as document release...16 Oct 2019 — Parks department says &quot;there is no evidence to confirm th...</span></span></span>

The survival story gained momentum because Tasmania still contains vast tracts of rugged forest and remote country. For believers, these landscapes offer the possibility that a small population could have persisted beyond the attention of scientists. For sceptics, the same landscapes explain why fleeting observations can be difficult to verify and easy to misinterpret.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.australiangeographic.com.au/nature-wildlife/2024/01/is-the-truth-still-out-there/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: australiangeographic.com.au">[Australian Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">australiangeographic.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">is the truth still out there</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Australian GeographicIs the truth still out there?16 Jan 2024 — The last confirmed direct evidence of thylacines in the wild was one capt...</span></span></span>

What separates the thylacine from most cryptid traditions is that even some researchers have argued that extinction may have occurred later than commonly assumed. A 2023 analysis of more than a thousand reported sightings suggested the species may have survived in isolated regions into the late twentieth century, perhaps even into the 1980s. Importantly, the study did not prove survival today; it proposed that extinction may have been delayed rather than occurring immediately after the last confirmed records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048969723014948" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[sciencedirect.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We compiled an exhaustive record of later possible sightings to test this...Read more...</span></span></span>

## The Real Animal Behind the Mystery

The emotional power of modern sightings comes from the thylacine’s documented history. Australians know exactly what is supposed to be out there. Film footage, museum specimens and photographs provide a clear reference point, unlike folklore creatures whose appearance changes from one account to another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thylacine" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The last known of its species died in 1936 at Hobart Zoo in Tasmania. That familiarity cuts both ways. Supporters of survival claims argue that experienced bush workers, park staff and hunters should be capable of recognising a thylacine when they see one. Critics counter that familiarity can create expectation: once a person knows the famous image of the Tasmanian tiger, an unusual glimpse of another animal may be unconsciously fitted into that template.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2023/mar/27/tasmanian-tiger-may-have-survived-into-the-00s-new-analysis-suggests" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianTasmanian tiger may have survived into the 2000s, new...27 Mar 2023 — Brook said the 1936 death of a thylacine at a Hobart z...</span></span></span>

The thylacine has also become a symbol of environmental loss. Its extinction is widely viewed as a human-caused tragedy. Because of that, reports of survival often carry an element of hope: the possibility that one of Australia's most famous lost animals somehow escaped the fate recorded in history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2018/07/02/the-obsessive-search-for-the-tasmanian-tiger" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newyorker.com">[The New Yorker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newyorker.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The New Yorker The Obsessive Search for the Tasmanian Tiger</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thought to be extinct since the 1930s following widespread hunting and habitat destruction, numerous sightings have been reported and uno...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2018</span></span></span>

## Extinction, Memory and Later Sightings

Reports have emerged from both Tasmania and mainland Australia, although Tasmania remains the primary focus. Government records released in recent years revealed numerous reports collected over decades, including accounts from motorists, farmers, hikers and bush workers. Most remain anecdotal and unverified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2019-10-16/thylacine-sightings-in-tasmania-revealed-in-rti/11602970" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">thylacine sightings in tasmania revealed in rti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsTasmanian tiger sightings detailed as document release...16 Oct 2019 — Parks department says &quot;there is no evidence to confirm th...</span></span></span>

## Why People Keep Looking

The search for surviving thylacines combines science, conservation, nostalgia and adventure.

For some searchers, the attraction is biological. Extinction dates are often messier than public memory suggests. Species sometimes survive unnoticed in remote areas for years after they are presumed gone. This makes the idea of a hidden remnant population seem at least conceivable, even if unlikely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048969723014948" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We compiled an exhaustive record of later possible sightings to test this...Read more...</span></span></span>

For others, the appeal is cultural. The thylacine has become one of Australia's most recognisable lost animals. ## A Mystery Shaped by a Real Loss

Most cryptid stories ask whether a creature ever existed. The thylacine story asks a different question: whether extinction happened exactly when people think it did.

That distinction explains why the Tasmanian tiger remains one of Australia's most enduring mystery-animal subjects.
