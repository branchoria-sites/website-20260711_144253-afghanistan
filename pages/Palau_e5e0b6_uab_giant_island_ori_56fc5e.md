## Introduction

Among all of [Palau]({{ 'does-palau-have-its-own-mystery-monsters/' | relative_url }})’s traditional stories, none is more closely tied to the shape of the islands themselves than the legend of Uab. Unlike a modern cryptid story built around mysterious sightings or claims of an unknown animal, Uab belongs to the realm of origin mythology. He is remembered as a gigantic being whose endless hunger threatened everyone around him. In the most widely known versions of the tale, his death transforms his enormous body into the islands of Palau, turning a cautionary story about greed into an explanation for the landscape people see today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islandtimes.org/uab-and-milad-story-of-palau/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islandtimes.org">[islandtimes.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islandtimes.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uab and milad story of palau</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Island Times NewsUAB and MILAD Story of Palau5 Apr 2022 — Uab was an ordinary little boy who gobbled everything in sight and became too b...</span></span></span>

The legend occupies a special place in Palauan culture because it does two things at once. It explains where the archipelago came from and teaches a moral lesson about appetite, excess and the responsibilities people owe to their community. For that reason, Uab is often described as Palau’s most famous giant figure, yet he is not treated as a historical creature or a cryptid in the modern sense. He is a mythic ancestor of the landscape itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islandtimes.org/uab-and-milad-story-of-palau/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islandtimes.org">[islandtimes.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islandtimes.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uab and milad story of palau</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Island Times NewsUAB and MILAD Story of Palau5 Apr 2022 — Uab was an ordinary little boy who gobbled everything in sight and became too b...</span></span></span>

## The Giant Who Could Never Be Fed

Most versions of the story begin with an unusual child. Sometimes the giant is called Uab, Uwab or Huab; some traditions even describe the figure as female rather than male. Despite these differences, the central theme remains remarkably consistent: the child possesses an appetite that grows beyond all reason.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://southpacificlegends.blogspot.com/2018/08/the-creation-of-palau.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southpacificlegends.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southpacificlegends.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the creation of palau</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">4 Aug 2018 — The story tells how this selfish child brought both disaster and creation to Angaur, the world&#x27;s first island.Read more...</span></span></span>

As Uab grows, so does the burden placed on the people around him. Villagers must gather ever greater amounts of food to satisfy him. What begins as a family problem becomes a threat to an entire community. In many retellings, the giant consumes nearly everything available and demands constant attention. His hunger is not merely physical; it represents selfishness taken to an extreme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islandculturearchivalsupport.wordpress.com/2017/07/17/the-greedy-giant-and-palau/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islandculturearchivalsupport.wordpress.com">[wordpress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islandculturearchivalsupport.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Island TimeThe Greedy Giant and Palau &#124; Island Time - WordPress.comJuly 17, 2017 — 17 Jul 2017 — In Angaur Island, long ago, there was bo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 17, 2017</span></span></span>

The story survives because it speaks to a practical reality of island life. Small island communities depend on cooperation and careful use of resources. A character who takes more than everyone else combined becomes a powerful warning symbol. Palauan storytellers have long used Uab to illustrate how uncontrolled desire can damage both the individual and the wider community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.islandconservation.org/legends-stories-lessons-palau/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islandconservation.org">[islandconservation.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islandconservation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">legends stories lessons palau</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Island ConservationLegends, Stories, and Lessons from Palau29 Nov 2017 — Former Palau Program Manager Joyce Beouch explains how stories a...</span></span></span>

In some versions, the giant becomes so immense that the land itself struggles to support him. Other tellings focus on the fear and hardship experienced by villagers forced to keep feeding him. Although details vary, the narrative always moves toward the same conclusion: Uab’s appetite cannot continue indefinitely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbslearningmedia.org/resource/prcc12.sci.life.eco.modernuab/an-ancient-legend-teaches-climate-change-adaptation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbslearningmedia.org">[pbslearningmedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbslearningmedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">His appetite caused other problems,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PBS LearningMediaAn Ancient Legend Teaches Climate Change Adaptation...Uab, a boy who grew into a giant as he ate everything around him...</span></span></span>

## How Uab's Body Became the Archipelago

The most memorable part of the legend is the transformation of the giant into the islands of Palau. After Uab becomes too powerful and destructive, the people devise a way to stop him. Different versions describe different methods, but fire commonly plays a role. Once the giant is defeated, his vast body falls across the sea and is transformed into the land itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islandtimes.org/uab-and-milad-story-of-palau/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islandtimes.org">[islandtimes.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islandtimes.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uab and milad story of palau</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Island Times NewsUAB and MILAD Story of Palau5 Apr 2022 — Uab was an ordinary little boy who gobbled everything in sight and became too b...</span></span></span>

In many retellings, individual parts of Uab's body correspond to different islands or regions. The giant ceases to be a living threat and instead becomes the physical foundation of the country. The story therefore functions as an origin map, linking geography to memory and morality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islandtimes.org/uab-and-milad-story-of-palau/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islandtimes.org">[islandtimes.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islandtimes.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uab and milad story of palau</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Island Times NewsUAB and MILAD Story of Palau5 Apr 2022 — Uab was an ordinary little boy who gobbled everything in sight and became too b...</span></span></span>

This idea places Uab within a wider family of creation myths found across Oceania, where the bodies of gods, giants or supernatural beings become mountains, islands and coastlines. In Palau, however, the emphasis is unusually direct: the giant’s excessive consumption ultimately breaks him apart, and the resulting fragments become a homeland for future generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.izumi-syuppan.co.jp/LLO_PDF/vol_14/14-02.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: izumi-syuppan.co.jp">[和泉出版印刷株式会社]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">izumi-syuppan.co.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The first part (Myth of Uab) tells of an island formed in the sea, and in the second part (Myth of Milad)...Read more...</span></span></span>

Some traditional interpretations go even further. Museum records and scholarly summaries note versions in which parts of the giant’s remains produce features of the natural world, while other details explain why certain areas are forested or inhabited. These additions transform the legend from a simple creation story into a broader explanation of how the world came to look the way it does.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/525606860/AN1013531X-v30-p113-125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It summarizes 74 legends in three groups.Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why Different Versions of the Story Survive

Readers searching for a single “correct” version of the Uab legend will quickly discover that none exists. Oral traditions change as they are retold. Storytellers emphasise different lessons, local places and family histories, producing multiple versions that share a common core while differing in detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/525606860/AN1013531X-v30-p113-125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It summarizes 74 legends in three groups.Read more...</span></span></span>

One important variation concerns Uab’s identity. Some traditions describe a male giant, while others refer to a female giant known as Chuab or a closely related figure. The broad narrative remains the same: a being grows beyond normal limits, dies, and becomes the land. The existence of these parallel versions reflects the flexibility of oral storytelling rather than disagreement about a historical event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://southpacificlegends.blogspot.com/2018/08/the-creation-of-palau.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southpacificlegends.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southpacificlegends.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the creation of palau</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">4 Aug 2018 — The story tells how this selfish child brought both disaster and creation to Angaur, the world&#x27;s first island.Read more...</span></span></span>

Scholars studying Palauan mythology have also noted that the Uab story often forms only one part of a larger creation sequence. In some accounts it is paired with the story of Milad and other foundational myths that explain how the islands, people and social order emerged. Rather than standing alone, Uab occupies a key position within a connected mythological history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.izumi-syuppan.co.jp/LLO_PDF/vol_14/14-02.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: izumi-syuppan.co.jp">[和泉出版印刷株式会社]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">izumi-syuppan.co.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The first part (Myth of Uab) tells of an island formed in the sea, and in the second part (Myth of Milad)...Read more...</span></span></span>

The survival of multiple versions is one reason the legend remains culturally important.
