## Introduction

The koolakamba is one of [Gabon]({{ 'what-monsters-hide-in-gabons-forest/' | relative_url }})’s strangest animal mysteries, but unlike river-monster stories, it centres on the forests and great apes of Central Africa. In the nineteenth century, explorers and naturalists described an ape that seemed to stand somewhere between a chimpanzee and a gorilla. It was said to have a flatter face, a larger skull, powerful jaws, unusual vocalisations and a tendency to walk more upright than ordinary chimpanzees. Some writers even suggested it was a natural hybrid between the two great apes. Yet after more than 150 years of discussion, no verified specimen, genetic evidence or accepted scientific classification has confirmed the koolakamba as a separate animal. Today, most researchers view the story as a mixture of misunderstood ape variation, taxonomic confusion and the limitations of early exploration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koolakamba" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 2, 2025 — The Koolakamba or Kooloo-Kamba is a purported hybrid species of chimpanzees and gorillas. ## Was The Koolakamba A Real Mystery Ape?

The koolakamba entered Western zoological literature during the era when Central African wildlife was still poorly known to European science. Gabon was particularly important because it was one of the regions where explorers encountered gorillas and chimpanzees in the wild for the first time.

The figure most closely associated with the creature is the explorer Paul du Chaillu, who travelled through Gabon between 1856 and 1859 and became famous for bringing gorilla specimens to Europe and North America. His reports helped convince many scientists that gorillas were real animals rather than travellers’ legends, but some of his claims were also controversial and heavily scrutinised by other naturalists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/paul-du-chaillu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lindahall.org">[lindahall.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lindahall.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Linda Hall Library Paul Du Chaillu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Linda Hall LibraryPaul Du Chaillu - The Linda Hall31 Jul 2017 — Paul Belloni Du Chaillu, a French/American explorer and adventurer, was b...</span></span></span>

Among the animals he described was an ape he called the kooloo-kamba or koolakamba. Du Chaillu regarded it as distinct from both the common chimpanzee and the gorilla. According to his descriptions, local hunters recognised it as a separate forest ape and identified it partly through its call, from which its name was supposedly derived.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://strangeanimalspodcast.blubrry.net/2020/06/29/episode-178-the-koolakamba/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: strangeanimalspodcast.blubrry.net">[strangeanimalspodcast.blubrry.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">strangeanimalspodcast.blubrry.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">That&#x27;s what a koolakamba is said to be, a chimp-gorilla hybrid.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Episode 178: The Koolakamba - Strange Animals Podcast29 Jun 2020 — Some people suggested the ape was a hybrid of a chimpanzee and a gorilla...</span></span></span>

The problem is that the evidence never advanced much beyond descriptions, skull measurements and a handful of disputed specimens. Unlike the gorilla, which was soon documented repeatedly and studied in detail, the koolakamba never became an accepted species.

## Paul du Chaillu's Gabon Specimen Story

Du Chaillu believed he had obtained specimens that demonstrated the existence of an unusual ape. He compared skulls and physical features and argued that the animal possessed characteristics that differed from known chimpanzees. In his accounts, the creature appeared larger, broader-faced and more heavily built than the chimpanzees familiar to European collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koolakamba" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

His claims arrived during a period when zoologists were still working out the relationships between Africa’s great apes. Scientists had relatively few specimens, many animals were known from incomplete remains, and the range of variation within ape populations was poorly understood. In that environment, unusual individuals could appear far more significant than they would today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/paul-du-chaillu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lindahall.org">[Linda Hall Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lindahall.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Linda Hall Library Paul Du Chaillu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Linda Hall LibraryPaul Du Chaillu - The Linda Hall31 Jul 2017 — Paul Belloni Du Chaillu, a French/American explorer and adventurer, was b...</span></span></span>

Du Chaillu's reputation also complicated matters. Although many of his observations proved valuable, critics questioned some aspects of his narratives and methods. The resulting debate meant that claims about extraordinary animals from his expeditions were often viewed with caution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/paul-du-chaillu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lindahall.org">[Linda Hall Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lindahall.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Linda Hall Library Paul Du Chaillu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Linda Hall LibraryPaul Du Chaillu - The Linda Hall31 Jul 2017 — Paul Belloni Du Chaillu, a French/American explorer and adventurer, was b...</span></span></span>

## The Ape Features That Made The Claim Unusual

Descriptions of the koolakamba varied between authors, which is one reason the mystery has remained so difficult to evaluate. Nevertheless, several recurring traits appear in historical accounts.

Writers commonly described the animal as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Larger and more robust than a typical chimpanzee.
* Possessing a flatter or broader face.
* Having pronounced brow ridges and strong jaws.
* Showing a more upright posture.
* Living in smaller groups or sometimes alone.
* Producing distinctive calls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koolakamba" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

</div>

These features sounded intriguing because they seemed to combine traits associated with both chimpanzees and gorillas. To nineteenth-century observers, the creature looked like a possible intermediate form between the two species.

However, later investigators noticed a major problem: descriptions often contradicted one another. Some accounts emphasised a flat face, while others described a strongly projecting muzzle. Ear shape, body proportions and behaviour also varied depending on the observer. Such inconsistency makes it difficult to determine whether witnesses were describing a single distinct animal at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://culturologies.wordpress.com/2018/12/07/mystery-of-the-koolakamba/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturologies.wordpress.com">[Cody Moser&#x27;s Culturologies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturologies.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cody Moser&#x27;s Culturologies Mystery of the Koolakamba</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cody Moser&#x27;s CulturologiesMystery of the Koolakamba - Cody Moser&#x27;s Culturologies7 Dec 2018 — Described as a possible chimpanzee-gorilla h...</span></span></span>

## Hybrid Theories And Why They Attracted Attention

The most famous explanation proposed by believers was that the koolakamba represented a chimpanzee-gorilla hybrid.

At first glance, the idea seemed attractive. Chimpanzees and gorillas occupy overlapping parts of Central Africa, including regions near Gabon. An ape displaying a mixture of characteristics from both species appeared to fit the hybrid hypothesis. The theory also appealed to Victorian naturalists who were fascinated by classification and evolutionary relationships.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koolakamba" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 2, 2025 — The Koolakamba or Kooloo-Kamba is a purported hybrid species of chimpanzees and gorillas. This alleged hybrid ape specie...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 2, 2025</span></span></span>

The hybrid interpretation survived into the twentieth century and occasionally resurfaced whenever photographs or captive apes appeared unusually gorilla-like. A famous example involved a photograph of an unusual ape at Yaoundé Zoo in Cameroon during the 1990s. Some observers suggested it might represent a koolakamba because its facial structure seemed different from that of typical chimpanzees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koolakamba" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Yet the hybrid theory faces a major obstacle: there is no verified scientific evidence that chimpanzees and gorillas naturally produce offspring. Despite decades of field research, captive studies, genetics and observation of wild ape populations, no confirmed chimpanzee-gorilla hybrid has been documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://karlshuker.blogspot.com/2013/01/going-ape-about-koolookamba.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karlshuker.blogspot.com">[ShukerNature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karlshuker.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">going ape about koolookamba</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ShukerNatureGOING APE ABOUT THE KOOLOOKAMBA19 Jan 2013 — Not a single verified example of a hybrid between a chimpanzee and gorilla has e...</span></span></span>

## Why Most Scientists Doubt The Koolakamba

Modern sceptical explanations focus less on hidden species and more on the way early zoologists interpreted limited evidence.

The leading explanation is that koolakamba reports were based on unusually large or atypical chimpanzees. Chimpanzee populations vary considerably in size, skull shape, facial appearance and colouration. Some Central African chimpanzees are larger and darker than the individuals that early European scientists first examined. An observer unfamiliar with the full range of variation could easily conclude that a distinct species existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koolakamba" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Another issue involves nineteenth-century taxonomy. Researchers often classified animals from very small sample sizes. A handful of unusual skulls or skins could be interpreted as evidence for a new species or subspecies when they might simply represent natural variation, age differences or individual abnormalities. Later reviews of the koolakamba literature found that the supposed defining traits overlapped heavily with known chimpanzee characteristics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ethnobiology.org/sites/default/files/pdfs/JoE/4-1/Shea1984.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ethnobiology.org">[Society of Ethnobiology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ethnobiology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Society of EthnobiologyBETWEEN THE GORILLA AND THE CHIMPANZEEby BT SHEA · 1984 · Cited by 14 — Both forms are said to occur side by side...</span></span></span>

Some zoologists even proposed treating the koolakamba as a chimpanzee subspecies rather than a separate species. Yet this interpretation also struggled because the alleged form lacked a clear geographic range and was said to occur alongside ordinary chimpanzees in the same forests. That pattern does not fit modern definitions of subspecies particularly well.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ethnobiology.org/sites/default/files/pdfs/JoE/4-1/Shea1984.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ethnobiology.org">[Society of Ethnobiology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ethnobiology.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Society of EthnobiologyBETWEEN THE GORILLA AND THE CHIMPANZEEby BT SHEA · 1984 · Cited by 14 — Both forms are said to occur side by side...</span></span></span>

The broader history of African mystery apes also encourages caution. Other supposed giant or intermediate ape forms, such as the famous Bili ape of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, eventually turned out to be populations of already known chimpanzees once genetic testing became available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bili_ape" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bili ape</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bili ape</span></span></span>

## How Koolakamba endured in local tradition

The koolakamba survives because it sits at the intersection of real wildlife and historical uncertainty. Unlike purely mythical creatures, it was linked to actual specimens, actual forests and genuine scientific debate. The question was never whether great apes existed in Gabon; it was whether one of those apes represented something previously unknown.

That ambiguity gives the story lasting appeal. The forests of Gabon remain among the most biologically rich landscapes in Africa, and the image of an ape that appears halfway between a chimpanzee and a gorilla is memorable. Yet the evidence accumulated over more than a century points away from a hidden species and toward a simpler explanation: early explorers encountered the natural diversity of Central African chimpanzees and interpreted some unusual individuals as something entirely new.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koolakamba" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 2, 2025 — The Koolakamba or Kooloo-Kamba is a purported hybrid species of chimpanzees and gorillas. For that reason, the koolakamba occupies an unusual place in Gabon’s mystery-animal history.
