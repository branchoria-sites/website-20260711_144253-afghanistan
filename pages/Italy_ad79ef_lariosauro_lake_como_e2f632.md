## Introduction

The Lariosauro of Lake Como is often presented as [Italy]({{ 'what-monsters-haunt-italys-lakes-and/' | relative_url }})’s answer to the Loch Ness Monster, a mysterious reptilian creature supposedly lurking in the deep waters of one of Europe’s most famous lakes. Yet the most interesting thing about the Lariosauro is not the evidence for an unknown animal. It is the evidence for a newspaper-created monster.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Italy_ad79ef_lariosauro_lake_como_e2f632-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Lariosauro illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Italy_ad79ef_lariosauro_lake_como_e2f632-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Italy_ad79ef_lariosauro_lake_como_e2f632-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Unlike many lake-monster traditions that evolved gradually from centuries of folklore, the Lariosauro can be traced with unusual clarity to a burst of post-war press coverage in 1946. A dramatic report, a prehistoric-sounding name, follow-up stories in local newspapers and a handful of later sightings combined to create a legend that has survived for decades. The result is one of Italy’s clearest examples of how journalism, local geography and popular imagination can turn a doubtful sighting into a lasting monster tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

## The 1946 Hunters' Story

The modern legend begins on 18 November 1946. A local newspaper, *Corriere Comasco*, published an account claiming that two hunters had encountered a strange creature near Pian di Spagna, a marshy area at the northern end of Lake Como. The animal was described as large, reptilian and covered with reddish scales. According to the story, it appeared close to shore before disappearing back into the water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

The report arrived at a moment when Italy was emerging from the Second World War and newspapers were eager for attention-grabbing stories. The sighting quickly spread beyond the local area and was repeated in other publications. Within days, additional reports appeared, including claims that the creature had been seen elsewhere around the lake and even suggestions that earlier encounters had occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

What makes the Lariosauro unusual among monster legends is that sceptical researchers later examined the newspaper trail itself. Italian investigator Massimo Polidoro argued that the original article reads less like eyewitness testimony and more like a journalistic invention. He noted that follow-up stories also appear to have been created by newspapers rather than emerging from independently documented witnesses. According to this interpretation, the monster was effectively born in print.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

The story became so popular that newspapers soon began offering explanations for the creature. One article suggested that the “monster” might actually have been a sturgeon. Researchers later pointed out that this explanation appears to have been as unsubstantiated as the original monster report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

## Fossil Name, Modern Legend

The choice of name was crucial to the monster’s success.

“Lariosauro” comes from “Lario”, an older name for Lake Como, combined with the name of a genuine prehistoric reptile called *Lariosaurus*. Fossils of this Triassic marine reptile were discovered in the region during the nineteenth century, giving the area a real palaeontological connection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

This fossil connection gave the legend a veneer of scientific plausibility. Readers could easily imagine that a descendant of an ancient reptile might somehow have survived in the lake. The idea echoed a pattern already familiar from other lake-monster traditions around the world, where prehistoric imagery helps transform ordinary sightings into stories of living relics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

In reality, the fossil animal and the monster have almost nothing in common beyond the name. *Lariosaurus* lived roughly 240 million years ago in a marine environment that existed long before Lake Como itself formed. The legendary Lariosauro is a twentieth-century creature of newspapers, tourism and local storytelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

The name nevertheless proved remarkably durable. Once attached to the mystery, it gave the creature a distinctive identity and helped separate it from countless other unexplained-animal stories.

## Why Lake Como Was the Perfect Setting

Lake Como offered everything a monster legend needed.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.varennaholidays.it/blog/larrie-the-monster-of-lake-como/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: varennaholidays.it">[varennaholidays.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">varennaholidays.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Larrie: the monster of Lake Como!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BlogDecember 8, 2023 — 8 Dec 2023 — This marine reptile&#x27;s legend started in 1946 when two hunters in the north area of Lake Como, Pian di...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2023</span></span></span>

The lake is exceptionally deep, reaching more than 400 metres in places. Its steep mountains, changing weather and dark waters naturally encourage speculation about what might be hidden beneath the surface. Even without any monster stories, it is a landscape that feels mysterious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lariosauro" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The original sighting location added another ingredient. Pian di Spagna is not an open stretch of lake but a wetland reserve of reeds, channels and shallow water. Such environments are ideal for misidentifications because animals may be seen only briefly and under poor viewing conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.manospondylus.com/2022/01/lariosauro-rather-curious-cryptid.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: manospondylus.com">[manospondylus.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">manospondylus.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">lariosauro rather curious cryptid</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lariosauro: A rather curious cryptid25 Jan 2022 — The first time a monster was sighted in the lake was in 1946, when the local newspaper...</span></span></span>

Just as importantly, post-war newspapers were able to spread local rumours quickly. Once the monster acquired a name and a backstory, every unusual observation on the lake could potentially become another Lariosauro sighting. The creature moved from a single report to a recognised local legend in a remarkably short period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

## Otters, Pike and Newspaper Invention

Later sightings are often cited as evidence that something mysterious really inhabited Lake Como. However, the details tend to point in the opposite direction.

In 1954, a witness at Argegno described an animal only around 80–90 centimetres long with a rounded muzzle, a broad rear section and webbed feet. Researcher Giorgio Castiglioni concluded that the description fits an otter surprisingly well. Otters were still present around the lake at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.massimopolidoro.com/misteri/un-mostro-nel-lago-di-como.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: massimopolidoro.com">[massimopolidoro.com-80.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">massimopolidoro.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">un mostro nel lago di como</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?7 Jun 2007 — Il mostro, con riferimento al nome del lago di Como (Lario), fu chiamato “lariosauro”, un nome che era stato dato nel XIX s...</span></span></span>

Another famous report appeared in 1957 when newspapers described a huge creature between Dongo and Musso. ## How a Newspaper Monster Became a Local Legend

The Lariosauro survived because it offered something more entertaining than a straightforward explanation.

A deep alpine lake, genuine prehistoric fossils, dramatic newspaper headlines and occasional strange sightings created a story that local people enjoyed repeating. Over time the monster became part of Lake Como’s cultural landscape. It appeared in books, local exhibitions, tourism material and popular culture, even as sceptical investigations increasingly pointed toward mundane explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.varennaholidays.it/blog/larrie-the-monster-of-lake-como/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: varennaholidays.it">[varennaholidays.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">varennaholidays.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Larrie: the monster of Lake Como!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BlogDecember 8, 2023 — 8 Dec 2023 — This marine reptile&#x27;s legend started in 1946 when two hunters in the north area of Lake Como, Pian di...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2023</span></span></span>
