## Introduction

The Nabau is one of [Malaysia]({{ 'what-monsters-haunt-malaysias-wild/' | relative_url }})’s most famous legendary creatures, but it is also one of the easiest to misunderstand. To many people outside Borneo, the name became known through a dramatic 2009 photograph that appeared to show a colossal serpent gliding through a river in Sarawak. Yet the Nabau did not begin as an internet monster. Long before viral images and social media debates, it existed in Iban tradition as a powerful water-dragon associated with rivers, protection, spiritual power and ceremonial art. The story of the Nabau is therefore not simply a tale about a giant snake. It is also a case study in how an Indigenous tradition became merged with modern hoax culture and online cryptid folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museum.sarawak.gov.my/web/subpage/webpage_view/295" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museum.sarawak.gov.my">[Sarawak Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museum.sarawak.gov.my</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sarawak MuseumThe nabau or water dragon among the Iban of Sarawakby M Janowski · 2019 · Cited by 8 — The nabau has been woven into pua cl...</span></span></span>

## Nabau in Iban River and Textile Tradition

In Iban tradition in Sarawak, the Nabau is generally described as a great water serpent or water dragon linked to rivers and the unseen world. Rather than functioning as a hidden animal waiting to be discovered by zoologists, it belongs to a wider spiritual landscape in which rivers, forests and powerful beings overlap. Academic studies of Iban belief describe the Nabau as a protective figure whose symbolism extends into ritual life and material culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museum.sarawak.gov.my/web/subpage/webpage_view/295" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museum.sarawak.gov.my">[Sarawak Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museum.sarawak.gov.my</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sarawak MuseumThe nabau or water dragon among the Iban of Sarawakby M Janowski · 2019 · Cited by 8 — The nabau has been woven into pua cl...</span></span></span>

One of the most important clues to the Nabau’s traditional role is found in textiles. The creature appears in ceremonial woven cloths and other textile designs, where it is associated with protection and spiritual potency. Researchers have noted that Nabau motifs can represent powerful beings connected to the heroic and semi-spiritual world of Iban tradition. These images were not intended as wildlife illustrations. They functioned as cultural symbols carrying meaning, status and protective significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museum.sarawak.gov.my/web/subpage/webpage_view/295" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museum.sarawak.gov.my">[Sarawak Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museum.sarawak.gov.my</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sarawak MuseumThe nabau or water dragon among the Iban of Sarawakby M Janowski · 2019 · Cited by 8 — The nabau has been woven into pua cl...</span></span></span>

The connection between the Nabau and giant snakes is not accidental. Studies of Iban textile symbolism note that snake motifs are often linked with enormous pythons and other powerful reptiles. In folklore, the boundary between an exceptionally large snake and a supernatural river dragon is often fluid. The Nabau occupies that ambiguous space, combining familiar animal traits with legendary attributes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.saspublishers.com/article/17873/download/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saspublishers.com">[SAS Publishers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saspublishers.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAS PublishersThe Iban&#x27;s Belief towards the Meaning of Pua Kumbu&#x27;s MotifSeptember 16, 2023 — The belief on the snake motif (figure 2) is...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 16, 2023</span></span></span>

For generations, this tradition remained largely local. The Nabau was known within Sarawak’s cultural landscape, especially among Iban communities, but it was not a globally recognised monster in the way that Loch Ness or Bigfoot had become.

## The 2009 Giant Snake Photograph Story

That changed dramatically in February 2009.

News outlets and websites began circulating a photograph allegedly taken from a helicopter over the Baleh River region of Sarawak during flood-monitoring operations. The image appeared to show an immense snake-like shape moving through a river surrounded by rainforest. Reports quickly linked the object to the legendary Nabau and repeated claims that the creature could exceed 100 feet in length and possess a dragon-like head.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.mongabay.com/2009/02/photos-of-100-foot-monster-snake-surface/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.mongabay.com">[Mongabay News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.mongabay.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Photos of &#x27;100-foot monster snake&#x27; surface</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nabau&#x27; snake, which is said to be more than 100-feet long with a dragon&#x27;s head. The photograph spread rapidly because it seemed to provide exactly what cryptid enthusiasts often seek: a dramatic image attached to an existing legend. For many international readers, the photograph was their first encounter with the Nabau. The folklore and the image became fused together almost overnight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.mongabay.com/2009/02/photos-of-100-foot-monster-snake-surface/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.mongabay.com">[Mongabay News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.mongabay.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Photos of &#x27;100-foot monster snake&#x27; surface</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nabau&#x27; snake, which is said to be more than 100-feet long with a dragon&#x27;s head. Several factors made the story especially appealing:

* It appeared to come from an aerial survey rather than an anonymous eyewitness.
* It was linked to a genuine river system in remote Borneo rainforest.
* The Nabau already existed in local folklore, giving the image a ready-made explanation.
* The apparent scale of the creature seemed far beyond that of known pythons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.mongabay.com/2009/02/photos-of-100-foot-monster-snake-surface/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.mongabay.com">[Mongabay News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.mongabay.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Photos of &#x27;100-foot monster snake&#x27; surface</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nabau&#x27; snake, which is said to be more than 100-feet long with a dragon&#x27;s head. As often happens with viral monster photographs, the image travelled much faster than attempts to verify it.

## Why Many Researchers Doubted the Image

Scepticism emerged almost immediately.

Observers noted that the river in the photograph did not resemble the appearance of the Baleh River described in local reports. Critics also pointed to odd-looking water ripples and inconsistencies in the image that suggested digital manipulation. Some analysts argued that the proportions made little sense: if the surrounding trees were used as scale references, the alleged serpent would be vastly larger than even the most extravagant folklore descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.mongabay.com/2009/02/photos-of-100-foot-monster-snake-surface/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.mongabay.com">[Mongabay News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.mongabay.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Photos of &#x27;100-foot monster snake&#x27; surface</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nabau&#x27; snake, which is said to be more than 100-feet long with a dragon&#x27;s head. The photograph shows a snake-like object in a blackwater...</span></span></span>

Another issue was the absence of supporting evidence. No additional photographs, physical traces, biological samples or independent observations accompanied the image. A creature large enough to match the photograph would represent a previously unknown giant reptile far exceeding the size of documented pythons. Yet no corresponding zoological evidence appeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.mongabay.com/2009/02/photos-of-100-foot-monster-snake-surface/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.mongabay.com">[Mongabay News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.mongabay.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Photos of &#x27;100-foot monster snake&#x27; surface</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nabau&#x27; snake, which is said to be more than 100-feet long with a dragon&#x27;s head. ## Pythons, Fakery and the Limits of Belief

The modern Nabau story sits at the intersection of three different ideas that are often confused with one another.

The first is traditional folklore. In this context, the Nabau functions as a meaningful cultural figure rooted in Iban belief and artistic tradition. Its importance does not depend on biological proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://museum.sarawak.gov.my/web/subpage/webpage_view/295" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museum.sarawak.gov.my">[Sarawak Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museum.sarawak.gov.my</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sarawak MuseumThe nabau or water dragon among the Iban of Sarawakby M Janowski · 2019 · Cited by 8 — The nabau has been woven into pua cl...</span></span></span>

The second is the possibility of misidentified wildlife. Borneo is home to large reticulated pythons, among the longest snakes on Earth. Encounters with unusually large specimens can easily feed stories about gigantic serpents, especially in riverine environments where size estimates become unreliable. A genuine python seen briefly from a distance may grow in the retelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.saspublishers.com/article/17873/download/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saspublishers.com">[SAS Publishers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saspublishers.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAS PublishersThe Iban&#x27;s Belief towards the Meaning of Pua Kumbu&#x27;s MotifSeptember 16, 2023 — The belief on the snake motif (figure 2) is...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 16, 2023</span></span></span>

The third is outright fakery or image manipulation. The 2009 photograph became famous largely because it appeared to visualise a creature that people already wanted to believe might exist.
