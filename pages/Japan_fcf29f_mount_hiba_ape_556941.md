## Introduction

The Mount Hiba ape, usually known as the Hibagon, is [Japan]({{ 'why-japan-keeps-finding-monsters/' | relative_url }})’s closest equivalent to the North American Bigfoot. Unlike many older Japanese monster traditions, however, the Hibagon emerged from a relatively modern wave of eyewitness reports. Beginning in 1970 around Mount Hiba in Hiroshima Prefecture, residents reported seeing a hairy, ape-like creature walking upright through forests, fields and mountain foothills. The story attracted national media attention, amateur investigators and curious tourists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pinktentacle.com/2008/05/seven-mysterious-creatures-of-japan/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pinktentacle.com">[pinktentacle.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pinktentacle.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">seven mysterious creatures of japan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">27 May 2008 — The first known Hibagon sighting occurred on July 20, 1970 in the area around Mt. What makes the Mount Hiba case especially interesting is that it sits on the boundary between mystery and misidentification. Witnesses genuinely reported seeing something unusual, yet the region already contained animals capable of producing confusing sightings under poor conditions. As a result, the Hibagon became less a question of whether a hidden ape lived in Japan and more a case study in how wildlife, local folklore and media excitement can combine to create a lasting legend.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yokai.com/hibagon/?srsltid=AfmBOorFATxHQf0ByrVyp16jQv8xN-ZDimn03RLWJlPv5QVhNFSHDZBC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yokai.com">[Yokai]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yokai.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HibagonThe first reported hibagon sightings took place in what is now Shōbara City, Hiroshima Prefecture in July of 1970. actually l...</span></span></span>

## What Was Seen On Mount Hiba?

Descriptions of the creature varied, but several details appeared repeatedly. Witnesses typically described a stocky, hairy figure between roughly 1.5 and 1.6 metres tall, covered in dark or reddish-brown hair, with a broad face, large eyes and a strong smell. Unlike the giant Bigfoot of North American lore, the Hibagon was usually portrayed as relatively small and shy. Witnesses often reported that it fled when approached.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yokai.com/hibagon/?srsltid=AfmBOorEaq9l5mjSRSFcpEzYFDIjOm5Au2y1pn4qwK9kRvH9atpqScH4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yokai.com">[yokai.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yokai.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HibagonHibagon are anthropoid apes which supposedly live in the vicinity of Mount Hiba in the Chūgoku Mountains. The creature became associated with the forests surrounding Mount Hiba, a mountain within the Hiba-Dogo-Taishaku region of western Honshu. The area contains dense woodland, rugged slopes and limited visibility in many places, conditions that can easily turn a brief glimpse of an animal into a puzzling encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hiba_%28Hiroshima%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mount Hiba (Hiroshima</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mount Hiba (Hiroshima</span></span></span>

## The Early 1970s Sighting Flap

The Hibagon story is unusually concentrated in time. Reports began in July 1970 and quickly multiplied. One of the earliest widely reported sightings came from the Mount Hiba area, followed by additional reports from nearby settlements and farmland. Contemporary accounts claimed that around a dozen sightings occurred during 1970 alone, with further reports continuing into the following years. Footprints and other traces were occasionally reported, although none provided conclusive evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pinktentacle.com/2008/05/seven-mysterious-creatures-of-japan/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pinktentacle.com">[pinktentacle.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pinktentacle.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">seven mysterious creatures of japan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">27 May 2008 — The first known Hibagon sighting occurred on July 20, 1970 in the area around Mt. Hiba. A total of 12 sightings were report...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 20, 1970</span></span></span>

A key turning point came when regional newspapers publicised the sightings. Media coverage transformed what might have remained a local curiosity into a national story. Researchers, journalists and university groups travelled to the area looking for evidence. The creature was given the memorable nickname “Hibagon”, derived from Mount Hiba itself, helping to establish a recognisable identity similar to Bigfoot or the Yeti.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibagon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The timing is important. The early 1970s were a period when international interest in creatures such as Bigfoot and the Yeti was already widespread. Japanese audiences were familiar with these stories, making the idea of a local ape-man easier to imagine and promote. The Hibagon was therefore both a local mystery and part of a broader global fascination with unknown hominids.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibagon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Bears, Macaques And Forest Visibility

The strongest sceptical explanations focus on ordinary wildlife rather than undiscovered apes.

### The Japanese macaque explanation

Many researchers have suggested that at least some sightings involved unusually large Japanese macaques. A macaque seen briefly while standing upright, especially at a distance or in poor light, can appear surprisingly human-like. Witnesses often estimate size inaccurately when observing unexpected animals in dense woodland. Several discussions of the Hibagon specifically identify oversized macaques as one of the leading explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yokai.com/hibagon/?srsltid=AfmBOorFATxHQf0ByrVyp16jQv8xN-ZDimn03RLWJlPv5QVhNFSHDZBC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yokai.com">[Yokai]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yokai.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HibagonThe first reported hibagon sightings took place in what is now Shōbara City, Hiroshima Prefecture in July of 1970. actually l...</span></span></span>

The weakness of this theory is that some reports described a creature larger than a typical macaque and with facial features that witnesses considered unlike any monkey they knew. However, eyewitness memory is not always reliable, particularly after media attention encourages repeated retelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://note.com/hitkawa/n/n369e8db21d16?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: note.com">[note（ノート）]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">note.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">note（ノート）A Strange Tale of Hibagon｜hitoshi kawamura... ape native to Japan&quot; becomes strong. ### The bear explanation

Another common explanation involves the Asiatic black bear. Bears inhabit the wider Hiba-Dogo-Taishaku region and can occasionally stand upright, creating a silhouette that resembles a human or ape. A bear partly obscured by vegetation may appear to have unusual proportions, especially if observed only briefly. Similar bear-based explanations have been proposed for other ape-like cryptids around the world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiba-Dogo-Taishaku_Quasi-National_Park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hiba-Dogo-Taishaku Quasi-National Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hiba-Dogo-Taishaku Quasi-National Park</span></span></span>

Yet the bear theory is not perfect either. Witnesses often reported seeing faces, eyes and movements they considered distinctly primate rather than bear-like. ### Why forests create strange sightings

The Mount Hiba environment itself may be part of the answer. Dense woodland limits depth perception and makes size estimation difficult. Brief encounters often occur when both observer and animal are startled. Under such circumstances, people tend to fill gaps in perception with expectations and assumptions. Once reports of a mystery creature become widely known, ambiguous sightings are more likely to be interpreted through that existing narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://note.com/hitkawa/n/n369e8db21d16?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: note.com">[note（ノート）]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">note.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">note（ノート）A Strange Tale of Hibagon｜hitoshi kawamura... ape native to Japan&quot; becomes strong. ## Why The Evidence Never Settled The Question

Unlike some cryptid cases that revolve around a single famous photograph or footprint cast, the Hibagon story consists largely of scattered witness testimony. Reports varied in appearance, size and behaviour. Physical evidence remained weak, and no specimen, clear photograph or biological sample ever emerged that could withstand scientific scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cryptomundo.com/cryptotourism/hibagon-japans-bigfoot/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cryptomundo.com">[cryptomundo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cryptomundo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">hibagon japans bigfoot</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revisiting the Hibagon – Japan&#x27;s Bigfoot7 Apr 2011 — The Hibagon is often described as looking like a gorilla or giant monkey, The Hibago...</span></span></span>

Another challenge is the short lifespan of the sighting wave. Although reports continued into the early 1980s, the concentration of sightings remained heavily tied to the early years of publicity. If a breeding population of large unknown primates had existed around Mount Hiba, researchers would generally expect stronger and more consistent evidence over time. The decline in reports after the initial excitement therefore supports sceptical interpretations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cryptomundo.com/cryptotourism/hibagon-japans-bigfoot/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cryptomundo.com">[cryptomundo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cryptomundo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">hibagon japans bigfoot</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revisiting the Hibagon – Japan&#x27;s Bigfoot7 Apr 2011 — The Hibagon is often described as looking like a gorilla or giant monkey, The Hibago...</span></span></span>

## How A Local Ape Became A Brand
