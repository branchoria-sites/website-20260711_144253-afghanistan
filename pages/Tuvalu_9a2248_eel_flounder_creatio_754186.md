## Introduction

How did an eel and a flounder make [Tuvalu]({{ 'where-are-tuvalus-monsters-and-mystery/' | relative_url }})? According to one of Tuvalu’s best-known origin stories, the islands, coconut palms and even the name of the country emerged from a struggle between two marine animals. Unlike a cryptid tale about a mysterious creature still lurking in the sea, this is a creation myth: a traditional explanation for why Tuvalu’s atolls are flat, why coconut palms are tall and slender, and how the islands themselves came into being. The story remains one of the most distinctive examples of how Tuvaluan folklore uses familiar reef animals to explain the landscape around them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. Within Tuvalu’s wider body of sea-centred traditions, the tale of the Eel and the Flounder stands out because it transforms ordinary marine creatures into the architects of an entire island nation. Rather than describing hidden beasts, it turns the shapes and behaviour of real animals into a memorable explanation of geography, identity and survival in a coral-atoll world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. ## The Contest with the Stone

In the most widely recorded version of the story, the Eel and the Flounder begin as friends. To settle a question of strength, they decide to carry a huge stone together. The test becomes an argument, and the argument becomes a fight. During the struggle, the stone crushes the Flounder, flattening its body. The Flounder retaliates by striking the Eel, injuring it so badly that it becomes thinner and thinner as it flees and eventually hides in a hole.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. The conflict does not end with the animals’ injuries. In many retellings, magical words are spoken that permanently fix their new shapes. The Flounder remains broad and flat, while the Eel remains long and narrow. Those transformed bodies then become the pattern from which parts of the visible world are formed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. The stone itself also acquires cosmic importance. After the struggle, the Eel throws it into the sky. Different versions describe the stone’s colours—often black, white and blue—becoming associated with darkness, light, sky and sea. Finally, the remaining stone is broken into pieces that become the islands of Tuvalu. Some tellings specifically connect the eight fragments to the traditional meaning of Tuvalu, often translated as “eight standing together”, reflecting the eight historically inhabited islands.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. ## Animals as Models for Islands and Palms

The most important feature of the myth is not the battle itself but the way animal bodies become a map of the environment.

The Flounder’s flattened form explains the appearance of Tuvalu’s low coral atolls. Seen from above, the broad shape of a flatfish provides an imaginative model for islands that barely rise above the sea. The story turns a familiar fishing-ground animal into a living explanation for the country's geography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. The Eel&#x27;s thin round body became the model for th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 5, 2025</span></span></span>

The Eel undergoes the opposite transformation. Its body becomes elongated, thin and cylindrical, qualities that are then associated with the coconut palm. Coconut trees are among the most important plants in Pacific island life, providing food, drink, fibre, building materials and fuel. By linking the coconut palm to the Eel, the myth connects one of Tuvalu’s most valuable resources directly to a marine ancestor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. This symbolic logic is characteristic of many Pacific origin stories. Instead of separating people from nature, the myth treats landscape, plants and animals as different expressions of the same creative process. The islands are not merely places where people live; they are living extensions of creatures known from the surrounding sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. ## Why an Eel and a Flounder?

The choice of animals makes sense in the environmental setting of Tuvalu. Both creatures are recognisable inhabitants of tropical marine ecosystems. Flatfish naturally suggest flat landforms, while eels possess long, serpentine bodies that resemble trunks, roots or vines. The story works because it begins with shapes that listeners already know.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. The eel is especially significant across Polynesia. Throughout the region, eels appear in myths explaining the origin of coconut palms. The famous Samoan story of Sina and the Eel, for example, tells how the first coconut tree grew from an eel’s remains. The Tuvaluan version follows a similar pattern but adapts it into a local explanation for both coconut palms and the islands themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sina_and_the_Eel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sina and the Eel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 27, 2025 — Sina and the Eel is a myth of origins in Samoan mythology, which explains the origins of the first coconut tree. This wider Polynesian background suggests that the Tuvaluan story belongs to a broad family of oceanic traditions while still retaining its own distinctive features. Instead of focusing on a human hero and a single coconut tree, it uses a contest between two marine animals to explain an entire archipelago.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. ## Moray Eels and the Story’s Natural Roots

For readers interested in cryptid traditions, the Eel can initially sound like a giant supernatural creature. Yet the tale is not presented in Tuvaluan tradition as evidence for a hidden species. It is an origin narrative built around animals that islanders would have encountered in daily life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvaluan_mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tuvaluan mythology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 5, 2025 — The Flounder&#x27;s flat body became the model for the atolls of Tuvalu. The Eel&#x27;s thin round body became the model for th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 5, 2025</span></span></span>

Moray eels, common on Pacific reefs, likely helped shape the imagery. Their elongated bodies, secretive behaviour and tendency to emerge suddenly from coral crevices make them striking animals. To people living on coral atolls, they are among the most memorable creatures in the reef environment. An intriguing cultural detail survives in some accounts: the importance of the Eel in the creation story contributed to a traditional respect for moray eels, which were regarded as tapu, or sacred, and not eaten in some communities.
