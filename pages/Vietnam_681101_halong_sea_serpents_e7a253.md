## Introduction

Hạ Long Bay is [Vietnam]({{ 'vietnam/' | relative_url }})’s most famous setting for sea-serpent stories. Unlike reports of mysterious creatures in remote forests, these tales emerged from a striking mix of local dragon mythology, colonial-era naval observations and later cryptozoological retellings. The result is a legend that sits halfway between folklore and maritime mystery. At its core is a series of reports by French sailors who claimed to have seen enormous serpent-like creatures moving through the waters of the bay around the turn of the twentieth century. Over time, those reports became linked to Hạ Long Bay’s dragon imagery, helping transform an isolated sighting story into Vietnam’s best-known sea-monster tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagevietnamairlines.com/en/the-legend-of-ha-long-bay/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagevietnamairlines.com">[Heritage Vietnam Airlines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagevietnamairlines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Vietnam Airlines The legend of Ha Long Bay</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Vietnam AirlinesThe legend of Ha Long BayFebruary 26, 2024 — 26 Feb 2024 — The story is summarized as follows: Junior Lieutenant...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 26, 2024</span></span></span>

The challenge for modern readers is that the story has changed repeatedly. Early accounts focused on unusual marine animals. Later versions emphasised dragons, prehistoric survivors and even giant marine centipedes. Understanding the legend means separating the original reports from the layers of myth and tourism that accumulated around them.

## Did Sailors See Dragons in Hạ Long Bay?

The most frequently cited sea-serpent reports come from French naval personnel operating in the Gulf of Tonkin during the colonial period. According to later summaries of the story, Lieutenant Lagrédin and members of the crew of the French vessel *Avalanche* reported seeing large serpent-like animals in Hạ Long Bay on several occasions between 1898 and 1902. Witnesses described elongated creatures moving through the water and estimated them to be many metres long.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagevietnamairlines.com/en/the-legend-of-ha-long-bay/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagevietnamairlines.com">[Heritage Vietnam Airlines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagevietnamairlines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Vietnam Airlines The legend of Ha Long Bay</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Vietnam AirlinesThe legend of Ha Long BayFebruary 26, 2024 — 26 Feb 2024 — The story is summarized as follows: Junior Lieutenant...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 26, 2024</span></span></span>

One reason these reports endured is that the descriptions differed from ordinary snakes. Retellings based on the crew’s observations claimed that the creatures moved with vertical undulations rather than the side-to-side motion expected from a large sea snake. Some accounts also mentioned a series of fin-like structures running along the body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pinebarrensinstitute.com/cryptids/2018/8/28/cryptid-profile-the-avalanche-sea-serpent-encounter" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pinebarrensinstitute.com">[THE PINE BARRENS INSTITUTE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pinebarrensinstitute.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cryptid profile the avalanche sea serpent encounter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE PINE BARRENS INSTITUTECryptid Profile: The Avalanche Sea Serpent Encounter29 Aug 2018 — The mystery creatures moved through the water...</span></span></span>

The original documentation is difficult to trace today, and many modern references rely on secondary retellings rather than surviving naval records. That makes it hard to determine exactly what was seen, how many witnesses were involved, and whether later versions exaggerated the details. Even so, the reports gained enough attention that they became attached to the identity of Hạ Long Bay itself. Several later histories and tourism accounts repeated the story as an explanation for why Europeans associated the bay with dragons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagevietnamairlines.com/en/the-legend-of-ha-long-bay/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagevietnamairlines.com">[Heritage Vietnam Airlines]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagevietnamairlines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Vietnam Airlines The legend of Ha Long Bay</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Vietnam AirlinesThe legend of Ha Long BayFebruary 26, 2024 — 26 Feb 2024 — The story is summarized as follows: Junior Lieutenant...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 26, 2024</span></span></span>

### Why the reports attracted attention

Several factors made the sightings memorable:

* The witnesses were presented as trained sailors rather than casual observers.
* The creatures were allegedly seen more than once over a period of years.
* The dramatic limestone landscape already encouraged associations with dragons and sea monsters.
* Sea-serpent reports were popular worldwide during the late nineteenth century, when newspapers regularly covered mysterious marine animals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritagevietnamairlines.com/en/the-legend-of-ha-long-bay/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritagevietnamairlines.com">[heritagevietnamairlines.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritagevietnamairlines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Heritage Vietnam Airlines The legend of Ha Long Bay</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heritage Vietnam AirlinesThe legend of Ha Long BayFebruary 26, 2024 — 26 Feb 2024 — The story is summarized as follows: Junior Lieutenant...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 26, 2024</span></span></span>

## The Marine Centipede Carcass Tale

A stranger branch of the legend developed around the so-called *Con Rit*, often translated in cryptozoological literature as a giant sea centipede. This story is separate from the French naval sightings but later became linked with them because both involved long, many-segmented marine creatures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://new-cryptozoology.fandom.com/wiki/Con_Rit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: new-cryptozoology.fandom.com">[New Cryptozoology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">new-cryptozoology.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New CryptozoologyCon Rit - New Cryptozoology Wiki - FandomOne such possible creature would be the Con Rit, which is Vietnamese for centipede...</span></span></span>

According to later retellings, nineteenth-century witnesses described enormous carcasses washed ashore along the Vietnamese coast. These remains were said to possess numerous fin-like projections, leading some writers to imagine a gigantic marine arthropod rather than a reptile. Over time, the alleged creature acquired labels such as “many-finned sea serpent” and “great sea centipede”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://new-cryptozoology.fandom.com/wiki/Con_Rit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: new-cryptozoology.fandom.com">[New Cryptozoology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">new-cryptozoology.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New CryptozoologyCon Rit - New Cryptozoology Wiki - FandomOne such possible creature would be the Con Rit, which is Vietnamese for centipede...</span></span></span>

The evidential problems are substantial. No preserved specimen is known, descriptions vary considerably, and many modern versions can be traced through cryptozoological books, websites and folklore compilations rather than contemporary scientific records. As often happens with sea-monster carcass stories, the absence of physical material makes independent evaluation impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://new-cryptozoology.fandom.com/wiki/Con_Rit" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: new-cryptozoology.fandom.com">[New Cryptozoology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">new-cryptozoology.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New CryptozoologyCon Rit - New Cryptozoology Wiki - FandomOne such possible creature would be the Con Rit, which is Vietnamese for centipede...</span></span></span>

A more mundane explanation is that observers encountered decomposing marine animals. Throughout the world, unusual carcasses have repeatedly generated sea-serpent rumours because decomposition can radically alter an animal’s appearance. Basking shark remains, for example, have often been mistaken for unknown monsters after the head and fins decayed away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuiyo-maru_carcass" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zuiyo-maru carcass</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zuiyo-maru carcass</span></span></span>

## How Dragon Lore Reshaped the Story

The sea-serpent legend became especially durable because it attached itself to a landscape already associated with dragons.

The name Hạ Long is commonly translated as “Descending Dragon”, and Vietnamese traditions describe dragons descending from heaven to defend the country and creating the bay’s spectacular islands and limestone formations. These stories existed independently of colonial sea-serpent reports, but the two traditions gradually blended in popular retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BA%A1_Long_Bay" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hạ Long Bay</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hạ Long Bay</span></span></span>

For European visitors, this fusion was irresistible. A bay whose name invoked dragons, filled with mist, caves and towering rock pillars, seemed like the ideal setting for a sea monster. As a result, later writers often treated the reported creatures as living dragons rather than unidentified marine animals. Tourism literature, travel writing and cryptid collections increasingly framed the sightings through the lens of mythic dragon imagery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.davestravelcorner.com/journals/destination-asia/the-halong-bay-monster/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: davestravelcorner.com">[davestravelcorner.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">davestravelcorner.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the halong bay monster</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">the halong bay monster</span></span></span>

The transformation changed the story in several ways:

* The focus shifted from specific witness reports to a broader dragon narrative.
* The creatures became symbols of place rather than zoological mysteries.
* The legend became easier to market to visitors interested in folklore and scenery.
* Details from separate monster traditions, including giant sea-centipede stories, were folded into a single Hạ Long Bay monster mythology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.davestravelcorner.com/journals/destination-asia/the-halong-bay-monster/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: davestravelcorner.com">[davestravelcorner.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">davestravelcorner.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the halong bay monster</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">the halong bay monster</span></span></span>

## What Might the Witnesses Have Seen?

No convincing evidence has emerged for an unknown giant marine animal living in Hạ Long Bay. There are no authenticated photographs, specimens, skeletal remains or modern scientific observations supporting the existence of a resident sea serpent. Several explanations have been proposed:

**Misidentified marine animals.** Large fish, whales, sharks, groups of porpoises, or animals viewed under unusual conditions can produce elongated silhouettes on the water's surface.

**Optical effects.** Waves, distance, haze and partial visibility can make separate animals appear to be one continuous creature.

**Story amplification.** Repeated retelling often increases estimated size, adds dramatic details and merges independent reports into a single narrative.

**Folkloric influence.** Witnesses and later audiences may have interpreted ambiguous observations through the cultural framework of dragons and sea monsters already associated with the bay.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tetzoo.com/blog/2021/2/16/the-lake-dakataua-migo-lake-monster-footage-of-1994" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tetzoo.com">[tetzoo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tetzoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the lake dakataua migo lake monster footage of 1994</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tetrapod ZoologyThe Lake Dakataua &#x27;Migo&#x27; Lake Monster Footage of 199417 Feb 2021 — The great classic work on sea monster accounts of 1968...</span></span></span>

What makes the Hạ Long Bay story interesting is not the strength of the evidence but the way different traditions converged. A handful of colonial-era marine sightings encountered one of Asia’s most visually dramatic landscapes and one of Vietnam’s best-known dragon legends. Together they created a sea-serpent tradition that has survived long after the original reports faded from public memory.

## Why the Legend Still Matters

Among Vietnam’s mystery-animal stories, the Hạ Long Bay serpent occupies a unique position. It is less a case of recurring modern sightings than a historical legend built from a small cluster of reports, local dragon traditions and later popular culture. The creature is sometimes called a sea serpent, sometimes a dragon, and sometimes a giant marine centipede. That flexibility has helped the story survive.

Today the legend functions as part of the cultural atmosphere surrounding Hạ Long Bay. Visitors come primarily for the limestone islands and UNESCO-recognised seascape, yet the idea that dragons or serpentine creatures once moved through those waters remains one of the most enduring pieces of folklore attached to the bay.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BA%A1_Long_Bay" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hạ Long Bay</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hạ Long Bay</span></span></span>
