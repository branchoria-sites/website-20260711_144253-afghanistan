## Introduction

Many of [Lebanon]({{ 'what-monsters-haunt-lebanons-stories/' | relative_url }})’s supposed “night beasts” become less mysterious when viewed through the realities of the country’s wildlife. Lebanon still supports a surprising range of carnivores, including striped hyenas, golden jackals, red foxes, wolves and the much rarer caracal. Most are nocturnal, secretive and encountered only briefly. A pair of glowing eyes on a mountain road, an odd silhouette crossing a rubbish dump, or unfamiliar tracks beside a village can quickly become a story about a monster-sized predator. In practice, the strongest explanation for many modern Lebanese beast reports is not an unknown animal but a known carnivore seen under poor conditions. The combination of darkness, rugged terrain and fleeting encounters creates ideal conditions for misidentification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257132274_Distribution_of_Striped_Hyaena_Hyaena_hyaena_syriaca_Matius_1882_Carnivora_Hyaenidae_in_urban_and_rural_areas_of_Lebanon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Hyaenidae) in urban and rural areas of Lebanon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 1, 2007 — Abi-Said &amp; Abi-Said (2007) reported the estimated population of striped hyenas globally to be less than 10...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2007</span></span></span>

## What Are People Really Seeing at Night?

Lebanon’s mountains, valleys and village edges place people unusually close to wildlife. Unlike countries where large wilderness areas separate humans from predators, Lebanon’s carnivores often move through landscapes shared with farms, roads, orchards and settlements. A surprising animal appearing in a headlight beam can therefore seem much larger, stranger or more threatening than it really is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257132274_Distribution_of_Striped_Hyaena_Hyaena_hyaena_syriaca_Matius_1882_Carnivora_Hyaenidae_in_urban_and_rural_areas_of_Lebanon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Hyaenidae) in urban and rural areas of Lebanon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 1, 2007 — Abi-Said &amp; Abi-Said (2007) reported the estimated population of striped hyenas globally to be less than 10...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2007</span></span></span>

* **Striped hyena** – Lebanon’s most misunderstood carnivore. Its sloping back, shaggy mane and nocturnal habits give it an unusual appearance even in daylight. In cryptid terms, these animals occupy an important middle ground. They are real, but uncommon enough that many people have never seen them clearly.

## Why Torchlight, Tracks and Silhouettes Distort Sightings

Night-time observation is notoriously unreliable. Human vision is poor at judging distance, colour and size in low light. Wildlife researchers frequently rely on camera traps and repeated observations precisely because a single glimpse can be misleading.

### The problem of size inflation

One of the most common errors is overestimating size. A fox standing on a ridge appears larger than a fox standing on flat ground. A hyena with its mane raised can seem dramatically bigger than its actual body size. Headlights or torches illuminating only part of an animal can also remove familiar visual cues that help people judge scale.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.royle-safaris.co.uk/striped-hyena-conservation-in-lebanon-17101/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royle-safaris.co.uk">[Royle Safaris &#124;]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royle-safaris.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">striped hyena conservation in lebanon 17101</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royle Safaris &#124;Striped Hyena Conservation in Lebanon24 Jun 2021 — The striped hyena (Hyaena hyaena) is Lebanon&#x27;s national animal as well...</span></span></span>

This helps explain reports describing animals as “wolf-sized”, “bear-like” or “larger than a dog” when the witness had only a few seconds to observe them.

### Eye shine and the illusion of something uncanny

Many nocturnal mammals produce strong eye shine when light reflects from structures behind the retina. In darkness, observers often see the glowing eyes first and the animal second. This can create the impression of a lurking creature long before its body is visible.

A fox, jackal or hyena partly hidden behind rocks may therefore appear as little more than two bright points moving through darkness. Once a witness begins guessing what produced the eyes, the mind tends to fill in missing details.

### Tracks tell a different story

Tracks often create their own mysteries. Canid footprints can be difficult for non-specialists to distinguish. A large dog, a jackal and a small wolf can leave impressions that look surprisingly similar under poor conditions.

Rain, erosion and rocky terrain make identification even harder. What begins as an unexplained set of prints can quickly become evidence for a much larger animal than was actually present.

## Why the Striped Hyena Creates So Many Monster Stories

Among Lebanese carnivores, the striped hyena is particularly well suited to generating beast legends. Studies have documented the species across much of Lebanon, including both rural and human-dominated landscapes. Yet it remains primarily nocturnal and is seldom seen clearly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257132274_Distribution_of_Striped_Hyaena_Hyaena_hyaena_syriaca_Matius_1882_Carnivora_Hyaenidae_in_urban_and_rural_areas_of_Lebanon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Hyaenidae) in urban and rural areas of Lebanon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 1, 2007 — Abi-Said &amp; Abi-Said (2007) reported the estimated population of striped hyenas globally to be less than 10...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2007</span></span></span>

The result is a classic cryptid mechanism: a real animal becomes stranger each time it is observed imperfectly.

## Where Villages, Rubbish, Roads and Rocky Slopes Create Encounters

The geography of Lebanese sightings matters as much as the animals themselves.

### Village edges and rubbish sites

Carnivores are attracted by food opportunities. Hyenas, foxes and jackals all exploit human waste to varying degrees. Encounters therefore tend to occur near settlement margins rather than deep wilderness. A resident expecting stray dogs may instead see an unfamiliar wild animal and assume something unusual is present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257132274_Distribution_of_Striped_Hyaena_Hyaena_hyaena_syriaca_Matius_1882_Carnivora_Hyaenidae_in_urban_and_rural_areas_of_Lebanon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Hyaenidae) in urban and rural areas of Lebanon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 1, 2007 — Abi-Said &amp; Abi-Said (2007) reported the estimated population of striped hyenas globally to be less than 10...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2007</span></span></span>

### Mountain roads after dark

Roadside sightings are especially prone to error. Drivers usually have only seconds to observe an animal crossing in front of headlights. Angles change rapidly, shadows move unpredictably and distance is difficult to judge.
