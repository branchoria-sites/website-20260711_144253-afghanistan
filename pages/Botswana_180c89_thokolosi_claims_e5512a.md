## Introduction

The thokolosi is probably the most recognisable monster figure in modern [Botswana]({{ 'what-monsters-haunt-botswanas-stories/' | relative_url }}), yet it occupies an unusual place in the country's folklore. Unlike a lake monster or mystery beast said to hide in remote wilderness, the thokolosi appears in villages, towns, newspaper advertisements, television dramas, courtroom claims, church deliverance sessions, and everyday rumours. The creature's importance lies less in evidence for its existence than in what people use it to explain: bad luck, mysterious illness, family conflict, unexplained events, jealousy, wealth, crime, and fear. In Botswana, the thokolosi sits at the boundary between folklore and contemporary life, making it one of the country's most persistent and visible modern monster traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mmegi.bw/features/i-have-a-thokolosi-for-sale/news" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mmegi.bw">[Mmegi Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mmegi.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mmegi OnlineI Have A Thokolosi For SaleFebruary 22, 2010 — 22 Feb 2010 — Dr Imrani, who also works with a lady acquaintance, Mama Aisha s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2010</span></span></span>

## Why Does the Thokolosi Still Scare Botswana?

Stories about the thokolosi have circulated across southern Africa for generations, but Botswana's modern encounters with the legend show how adaptable the figure has become. The creature is commonly described as a small supernatural being capable of carrying out the wishes of a master, causing disturbances at night, frightening households, or bringing misfortune. Whether believers view it as a spirit, a magical servant, or something else entirely, the thokolosi remains a familiar explanation when events seem difficult to understand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mmegi.bw/features/i-have-a-thokolosi-for-sale/news" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mmegi.bw">[Mmegi Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mmegi.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mmegi OnlineI Have A Thokolosi For SaleFebruary 22, 2010 — 22 Feb 2010 — Dr Imrani, who also works with a lady acquaintance, Mama Aisha s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2010</span></span></span>

What makes the Botswana version especially interesting is that many reports are not wilderness sightings. Instead, the creature appears in social settings:

* Families blame unusual events on a thokolosi.
* Religious groups conduct ceremonies intended to remove one.
* Traditional-healer advertisements claim to provide, control, or defeat one.
* Criminal defendants occasionally invoke the creature to explain wrongdoing.
* Television dramas use the figure as a symbol of fear and social conflict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mmegi.bw/features/i-have-a-thokolosi-for-sale/news" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mmegi.bw">[mmegi.bw]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mmegi.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mmegi OnlineI Have A Thokolosi For SaleFebruary 22, 2010 — 22 Feb 2010 — Dr Imrani, who also works with a lady acquaintance, Mama Aisha s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2010</span></span></span>

The result is a monster that functions as a cultural explanation rather than a hidden animal waiting to be discovered.

## What the Thokolosi Is Said to Be

Descriptions vary, but modern Botswana stories generally portray the thokolosi as a small, powerful supernatural being acting on behalf of a human owner. Reports often focus on what the creature supposedly does rather than what it looks like.

Common claims include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Carrying out instructions from a master.
* Bringing wealth or success through supernatural means.
* Harassing enemies.
* Causing frightening disturbances in homes.
* Appearing at night or remaining unseen while leaving effects behind.

</div>

One of the most revealing examples appeared in a widely discussed newspaper feature in 2010. A self-described healer openly advertised thokolosi for sale, claiming the beings could perform labour, help around the home, mould bricks, and work in fields. The story demonstrated not evidence for a creature, but evidence for the thokolosi's cultural visibility. Advertisers clearly believed enough people recognised the concept for it to be used as a commercial selling point.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mmegi.bw/features/i-have-a-thokolosi-for-sale/news" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mmegi.bw">[Mmegi Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mmegi.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mmegi OnlineI Have A Thokolosi For SaleFebruary 22, 2010 — 22 Feb 2010 — Dr Imrani, who also works with a lady acquaintance, Mama Aisha s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2010</span></span></span>

From a folklore perspective, that is significant. Many legendary creatures survive only in oral tradition. The thokolosi moved into public advertising, making it part of everyday media culture.

## Adverts, Television, and Newspaper Claims

The thokolosi's modern reputation owes much to mass media.

### The newspaper phenomenon

Botswana newspapers have repeatedly reported on healers who claim to possess special powers connected to thokolosi stories. The 2010 "I Have A Thokolosi For Sale" feature became notable because it documented how openly such claims were being marketed. Rather than hiding the idea, advertisers promoted it in the same space as other supernatural services such as love charms and fortune-related remedies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mmegi.bw/features/i-have-a-thokolosi-for-sale/news" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mmegi.bw">[Mmegi Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mmegi.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mmegi OnlineI Have A Thokolosi For SaleFebruary 22, 2010 — 22 Feb 2010 — Dr Imrani, who also works with a lady acquaintance, Mama Aisha s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2010</span></span></span>

These stories reveal less about monsters than about public demand. A legend remains commercially useful only if enough people take it seriously, fear it, or are fascinated by it.

### The television drama controversy

In 2006, Botswana Television aired a drama series titled *Thokolosi*. The programme focused on witchcraft themes and generated controversy among viewers. Academic analysis of audience reactions found that the series sparked debate about belief, culture, censorship, and representation. Some viewers objected to how communities were portrayed, while others saw the programme as an opportunity to discuss subjects often treated as taboo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/AJA18167659_73" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.co.za">[Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.co.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Batswana audience and the Thokolosi television drama...by PG Ebewo, Patrick J.* and Mmila · 2007 · Cited by 5 — In February 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2006</span></span></span>

Stories involving suspected thokolosi activity continue to circulate through newspapers, radio, social media, and community reporting. Accounts of mysterious fires, alleged hauntings, unexplained disturbances, or church interventions often attract significant public attention. These reports rarely provide verifiable evidence of a supernatural creature, but they demonstrate how the thokolosi remains a ready-made explanation for unusual events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thevoicebw.com/thokolosi-defeated/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thevoicebw.com">[thevoicebw.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thevoicebw.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">thokolosi defeated</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">12 Mar 2022 — After suffering the wrath of a &#x27;fiery creature&#x27; that wreaked havoc on their modest home for years, a family in Tobane has f...</span></span></span>

## When Folklore Becomes a Harmful Excuse

The most serious aspect of modern thokolosi rumours appears when folklore is used to explain harmful human actions.

A striking example emerged in a Botswana court case reported in 2026. A convicted man attempted to blame the rape of his young stepdaughter on a thokolosi rather than accepting responsibility for the crime. The court rejected the explanation and relied on witness testimony and supporting evidence instead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/TheVoiceBW/posts/in-a-desperate-attempt-to-save-his-skin-a-man-found-guilty-of-raping-his-11-year/1391762199439763/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In a desperate attempt to save his skin, a man found guilty...In a desperate attempt to save his skin, a man found guilty of rap...</span></span></span>

Cases like this illustrate an important distinction. Folklore can be culturally significant without being treated as a factual defence for criminal behaviour. Courts operate on evidence, and supernatural explanations generally fail when measured against witness accounts, forensic evidence, or established facts.

This is one reason the thokolosi attracts criticism from sceptics and community leaders. They argue that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Supernatural accusations can divert attention from real causes of problems.
* Vulnerable people may be blamed unfairly.
* Fear can spread through rumour networks.
* Serious crimes may be disguised behind folklore explanations.

</div>

The issue is not whether people believe the stories, but how those stories affect real decisions and real lives.

## The cultural afterlife of Thokolosi

Several factors help explain why the thokolosi remains influential in Botswana despite the absence of convincing physical evidence.

First, the creature offers a simple explanation for difficult situations. Illness, financial hardship, family disputes, and sudden misfortune often create a demand for answers.

Second, the legend is flexible. Unlike a creature tied to one lake, forest, or mountain, the thokolosi can appear anywhere. It adapts easily to changing social conditions.

Third, the figure exists within a broader environment where traditional healing practices continue to play an important role for many people. Botswana's public discussions about traditional medicine show that indigenous healing traditions remain culturally significant, creating a setting in which supernatural explanations continue to circulate alongside modern scientific and medical approaches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/69050" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dailynews.gov.bw">[DailyNews]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dailynews.gov.bw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Traditional healers call for collaboration with medicsSome traditional health practitioners have called for the integration of t...</span></span></span>

Finally, the thokolosi has become part of popular culture. Television dramas, newspaper stories, social-media discussions, and local rumours continually refresh the legend for new generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/AJA18167659_73" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.co.za">[journals.co.za]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.co.za</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Batswana audience and the Thokolosi television drama...by PG Ebewo, Patrick J.* and Mmila · 2007 · Cited by 5 — In February 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2006</span></span></span>

## A Monster of Rumour Rather Than Zoology

Among Botswana's strange-creature traditions, the thokolosi stands apart because it is not primarily a mystery animal. There are no tracks to analyse, no photographs that suggest an unknown species, and no biological evidence pointing to an undiscovered creature. Instead, the thokolosi survives as a social monster: a figure that appears whenever people seek explanations for fear, misfortune, conflict, or uncertainty.

That makes it one of Botswana's most revealing legends. The story persists not because anyone has demonstrated the existence of a hidden creature, but because the thokolosi continues to serve a powerful cultural role.
