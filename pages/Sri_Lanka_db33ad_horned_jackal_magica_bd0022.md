## Introduction

Among [Sri Lanka]({{ 'what-lurks-in-sri-lankas-wild-places/' | relative_url }})’s stranger animal legends, the horned jackal occupies an unusual place. Unlike stories about hidden monsters or unknown species, this tradition focuses on a supposedly magical object: a tiny “horn” said to grow from the head of an exceptionally rare golden jackal. For centuries, people in Sri Lanka believed that this horn, known as the *narri-comboo* or *nari kombu*, could bring luck, protect property, influence court cases and grant other advantages to its owner. The fascination was not with a new kind of animal but with a prized trophy taken from an otherwise ordinary jackal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://karlshuker.blogspot.com/2016/12/the-horned-jackal-and-narri-comboo-horn.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karlshuker.blogspot.com">[ShukerNature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karlshuker.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the horned jackal and narri comboo horn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meanwhile, further details concerning Sri Lanka&#x27;s perplexing horned jackal...Read more...</span></span></span>

Today the horned jackal sits at the boundary between folklore, natural history and cryptozoology. Reports of the object have circulated since at least the nineteenth century, yet no convincing evidence has emerged that Sri Lankan jackals regularly grow true horns. The story survives because it combines a real animal, a rare anatomical anomaly and a powerful tradition of magical belief.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://karlshuker.blogspot.com/2016/12/the-horned-jackal-and-narri-comboo-horn.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karlshuker.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karlshuker.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the horned jackal and narri comboo horn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meanwhile, further details concerning Sri Lanka&#x27;s perplexing horned jackal...Read more...</span></span></span>

## What the Supposed Jackal Horn Was

The legend centres on the Sri Lankan jackal, a local form of the golden jackal found across the island. According to traditional belief, the leader of a jackal pack occasionally developed a small horn-like projection hidden beneath the fur at the back of its head. The growth was generally described as only about half an inch long and therefore difficult to spot unless the animal was examined closely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://karlshuker.blogspot.com/2016/12/the-horned-jackal-and-narri-comboo-horn.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karlshuker.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karlshuker.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the horned jackal and narri comboo horn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meanwhile, further details concerning Sri Lanka&#x27;s perplexing horned jackal...Read more...</span></span></span>

Nineteenth-century naturalist Sir James Emerson Tennent recorded the belief in his writings on Ceylon (Sri Lanka). He noted that both Sinhalese and Tamil communities regarded the object as genuine and highly valuable. The “horn” was not imagined as a large antler or rhinoceros-style structure. Instead, it was a small bony or horny projection concealed by hair, making it mysterious and difficult to verify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://karlshuker.blogspot.com/2016/12/the-horned-jackal-and-narri-comboo-horn.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karlshuker.blogspot.com">[ShukerNature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karlshuker.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the horned jackal and narri comboo horn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meanwhile, further details concerning Sri Lanka&#x27;s perplexing horned jackal...Read more...</span></span></span>

The tradition became so widespread that some accounts claimed preserved examples existed. Later writers repeated stories that a skull bearing such a growth had once been housed in a British museum collection, although modern researchers have struggled to trace convincing documentation or photographs of such a specimen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dermestaria.com/412473_the-cryptic-horned-jackal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dermestaria.com">[dermestaria.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dermestaria.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The cryptic horned jackalMarch 19, 2020 — 19 Mar 2020 — Even today, a narric-comboo is greatly prized as a lucky talisman by Sri Lankans...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2020</span></span></span>

## Why It Became a Magical Trophy

The horn’s reputation mattered far more than its anatomy. In Sri Lankan folklore, possession of a *narri-comboo* was believed to confer extraordinary benefits. Different traditions credited it with protecting jewellery from theft, bringing wealth, granting wishes or helping its owner prevail in legal disputes. One nineteenth-century story even described a litigant who supposedly succeeded in court after obtaining the prized charm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cryptidarchives.fandom.com/wiki/Horned_jackal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cryptidarchives.fandom.com">[fandom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cryptidarchives.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cryptid ArchivesHorned jackal &#124; Encyclopaedia of Cryptozoology &#124; FandomThe &quot;narri-comboo&quot; of a horned jackal is greatly prized as a lucky...</span></span></span>

Because of these powers, the object became a sought-after talisman rather than a zoological curiosity. Its rarity increased its value. If only one jackal in a pack possessed the horn, and if the growth was hidden beneath fur, acquiring one would seem almost impossible. That scarcity helped transform the horn into a status object and magical treasure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://karlshuker.blogspot.com/2016/12/the-horned-jackal-and-narri-comboo-horn.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karlshuker.blogspot.com">[ShukerNature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karlshuker.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the horned jackal and narri comboo horn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meanwhile, further details concerning Sri Lanka&#x27;s perplexing horned jackal...Read more...</span></span></span>

The belief also connects Sri Lanka to a wider South Asian tradition. Similar stories appear in India, Nepal and Bangladesh, where “jackal horns” are sold as magical charms under various local names. Across the region, the object is associated with prosperity, protection and supernatural influence. The details vary, but the underlying idea remains remarkably consistent: a rare part of a jackal can bring unusual power to its owner.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackal%27s_horn" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jackal&#x27;s horn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jackal&#x27;s horn</span></span></span>

## Why People Believed the Story

The horned jackal legend persisted partly because it contained just enough biological plausibility to seem possible. Real animals occasionally develop unusual growths. Horn-like projections can arise through injury, infection, cysts, tumours or developmental abnormalities. A rare skull abnormality in a jackal could therefore have inspired stories that spread through hunting communities and rural folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_jackal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Golden jackal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Golden jackal</span></span></span>

Another factor is the hidden nature of the alleged horn. Because it was said to be covered by fur, most people would never expect to see one directly. This made the claim difficult to disprove. The rarity of the object also ensured that stories travelled more easily than specimens. In practice, almost everyone heard about jackal horns from someone else rather than discovering one themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://karlshuker.blogspot.com/2016/12/the-horned-jackal-and-narri-comboo-horn.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karlshuker.blogspot.com">[ShukerNature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karlshuker.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the horned jackal and narri comboo horn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meanwhile, further details concerning Sri Lanka&#x27;s perplexing horned jackal...Read more...</span></span></span>

As often happens with magical animal products, the legend created a market. Once people were willing to pay for a horn, there was an incentive for traders to offer substitutes. Reports from South Asia describe fake jackal horns fashioned from other animal parts, particularly small pieces of antler or bone, and sold to buyers seeking luck or protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackal%27s_horn" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jackal&#x27;s horn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jackal&#x27;s horn</span></span></span>

## Natural Explanations for an Impossible Feature

Modern zoology offers no evidence that golden jackals possess a normal, inherited horn-growing trait. Horns require specialised structures and developmental pathways that jackals, as members of the dog family, simply do not have. No recognised population of horned jackals has ever been documented scientifically.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_jackal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Golden jackal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Golden jackal</span></span></span>

The most plausible explanations fall into three broad categories:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Abnormal bone growths:** Rare bony protrusions can appear on mammal skulls because of injury, disease or developmental irregularities.
* **Misidentified specimens:** A damaged skull, attached fragment of bone or other unusual growth may have been interpreted as a horn.
* **Manufactured trophies:** In regions where magical value creates demand, animal parts can be altered or fabricated and then presented as genuine rarities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackal%27s_horn" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jackal&#x27;s horn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jackal&#x27;s horn</span></span></span>

</div>

## A Cryptid Legend Without a Cryptid

The horned jackal stands apart from most mystery-animal stories in Sri Lanka because the legend never required a hidden species. The animal itself was familiar. What mattered was the belief that an extraordinarily rare individual carried a magical object concealed within its fur. As a result, the story developed less as a search for an unknown beast and more as a search for a lucky charm.

That distinction makes the horned jackal a revealing example of how folklore and wildlife traditions interact. A common predator, a possible anatomical oddity and a strong belief in talismans combined to create one of Sri Lanka’s most enduring animal mysteries.
